## 1. Sources of engine noise, engine orders, typical spectra and levels

### Takeaway
For the exterior sound of a road engine the dominant source is exhaust orifice (tailpipe) noise, which is well modelled as an acoustic monopole whose strength is the time derivative of the unsteady mass flow leaving the pipe; intake orifice noise is the same mechanism with reversed flow, while combustion/structure-borne and mechanical noise (piston slap, valvetrain, gears, turbo) add higher-frequency, more broadband content radiated by the engine shell. Everything periodic is locked to engine orders: a 4-stroke cylinder fires once per two revolutions, so the cylinder event rate is order 0.5 and the whole-engine firing frequency is N_cyl·rpm/120.

### Cited Findings
- Exhaust radiation in single-cylinder engine studies has been computed by treating the tailpipe outlet as a monopole, with the pressure/flow history at the open end predicted by a 1D unsteady-flow model — [NASA NTRS 19690002231](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19690002231/downloads/19690002231.pdf) (attribution per search summary; full text not read).
- A summary of Kunz (1999) states that the sound pressure at any point beyond the exhaust termination "is a direct function of the instantaneous mass flow rate from the end of the exhaust pipe", scaled by source-microphone distance — [IJAV vol. 9 (2004) paper 396](https://iiav.org/ijav/content/volumes/9_2004_1722161272264886/vol_3/396_firstpage_415331286453860.pdf) (per search summary).
- The instantaneous mass flow across the exhaust valves is identified as the primary source that then propagates through the exhaust line — [SAE 2019-24-0174, "A Simple Approach for the Estimation of the Exhaust Noise Source at the Valves"](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/a-simple-approach-estimation-exhaust-noise-source-valves-2019-24-0174).
- Wall-modelled LES of a vehicle exhaust orifice found that a pulsating mass flow gave significantly higher SPL than a steady flow at higher mean Mach number, attributed to the pulsating flow acting as a monopole source — [FAU dissertation, "Entwicklung und Verifikation virtueller Methoden zur Bewertung des Mündungsgeräusches von Fahrzeugabgasanlagen"](https://open.fau.de/handle/openfau/21145).
- A steady subsonic jet has no macroscopic monopole and no external-force dipole; its sound comes from quadrupole turbulence sources (i.e., the steady part of the tailpipe flow contributes only weak broadband jet noise) — [MIT 16.50 lecture 36](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-50-introduction-to-propulsion-systems-spring-2012/3ef25ccfca81dc2240acd75d665c33e3_MIT16_50S12_lec36.pdf).
- Combustion noise (Austen & Priede, 1959): diesel noise is assigned tentatively to three mechanisms in three frequency ranges, with the range from about 800 Hz upward driven by cylinder pressure; abrupt combustion raises both the rate of pressure rise and the high-frequency spectral level of cylinder pressure — [Institute of Acoustics, D. Anderton, "I.C. engine combustion noise and methods for its reduction"](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/D%20ANDERTON%20I.C.%20ENGINE%20COMBUSTION%20NOISE%20AND%20METHODS%20FOR%20ITS%20REDUCTION.pdf); [SAE 590127 "Origins of diesel engine noise"](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/origins-diesel-engine-noise-590127).
- Anderton (1977) gives a prediction of combustion-induced noise as a function of rate of pressure rise, speed and bore applied to a "standard engine structure" — [IoA Anderton paper](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/D%20ANDERTON%20I.C.%20ENGINE%20COMBUSTION%20NOISE%20AND%20METHODS%20FOR%20ITS%20REDUCTION.pdf); Schaberg, Priede & Dutkiewicz (1990) classify cylinder pressure by rate of rise, peak and spectrum and discuss both combustion-chamber cavity and structure response — [SAE 900013](https://saemobilus.sae.org/papers/effects-a-rapid-pressure-rise-engine-vibration-noise-900013).
- Later work (HCCI, 2014) argues noise comes mainly from the structure responding to the impulse-like load of a fast pressure rise rather than cavity ringing, with a pressure-rise-rate threshold above which combustion noise dominates mechanical noise; structure eigenfrequencies strongly shape the forced response — [Int. J. Engine Research ref list, doi 10.1177/1468087414535598](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/ref/10.1177/1468087414535598); [Proc. IMechE K, doi 10.1243/14644193JMBD112](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1243/14644193JMBD112).
- Half order: the firing frequency of a single cylinder of a 4-stroke is the half engine order (half the revolution frequency); e.g., at 1050 rpm the 1st order is 17.5 Hz and the half order 8.75 Hz — [US Patent 10968842](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10968842).
- Whole-engine firing frequency for a 4-stroke = N_cyl × rpm / 120 — [Stainless Headers drone-frequency calculator](https://www.stainlessheaders.com/p-35905-drone-frequency-calculator.html) (commercial rule of thumb, consistent with the patent definition above).
- Half-order content is attributed to cylinder-to-cylinder variation (e.g., unequal-length headers) producing 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5… orders and a "burble"; changing firing order mainly affects the half orders — [eng-tips "3rd order noise" thread](https://eng-tips.com/threads/3rd-order-noise.129842); [Politechnika Krakowska dissertation](https://repozytorium.biblos.pk.edu.pl/resources/45168) (forum + dissertation via search summary).
- Regulatory exterior levels: UN ECE R51.03 pass-by (microphones 7.5 m from the track centreline) phase-1 limit 72 dB(A) from July 2016, falling to 68 dB(A) for M1 cars by 2024 (−6 dB over three phases); indoor pass-by approved for homologation since 2019 (R51.03 Am4) — [HBK/B&K "Indoor pass-by"](https://www.bksv.com/en/knowledge/blog/sound/indoor-pass-by-a-solution-to-an-outdoor-problem); [B&K "Pass-by noise regulations"](https://www.bksv.com/en/knowledge/blog/sound/pass-by-noise-regulations).

### Inferences
- **Order arithmetic (exact by definition).** Order n has frequency f_n = n·rpm/60. For a 4-stroke with N_cyl evenly firing cylinders the dominant exhaust order is N_cyl/2 (V8 → order 4; I4 → order 2; I6 → order 3). Example: V8 at 6000 rpm → firing 400 Hz; I4 at 3000 rpm → 100 Hz. Uneven firing (cross-plane V8 per bank, V-twins, odd-fire V6) or unequal runner lengths leak energy into half orders 0.5, 1.5, 2.5…, which is the perceptual "burble/lope". A synthesizer must therefore preserve per-cylinder identity (separate per-cylinder sources and paths) rather than a single firing-rate oscillator.
- **Monopole source strength [textbook, not re-verified; Kinsler et al. *Fundamentals of Acoustics* 4th ed. ch. 7; Pierce *Acoustics* ch. 4].** A compact pulsating source of volume velocity Q(t) in free field gives p(r,t) = ρ0/(4πr) · dQ/dt (t − r/c0). Written with mass flow: p(r,t) = 1/(4πr) · dṁ/dt (t − r/c0) (ṁ = ρQ at the orifice; only the fluctuating part radiates). Near a rigid ground plane (tailpipe ~0.3 m above road) the image source adds up to +6 dB at low frequency (half-space: 1/(2πr)).
- **Order-of-magnitude level check (my arithmetic, illustrative).** 2.0 L 4-cyl at 3000 rpm WOT: mean exhaust ṁ ≈ ρ·V_d·(rpm/120)·η_v ≈ 1.2·0.002·25·0.9 ≈ 0.054 kg/s. If the firing-frequency (100 Hz) component of tailpipe ṁ after a muffler is ±0.02 kg/s, dṁ/dt peak = 2π·100·0.02 ≈ 12.6 kg/s², p_peak(1 m) ≈ 12.6/(4π) ≈ 1.0 Pa → ≈ 91 dB SPL rms at 1 m; an unmuffled pipe with ±0.05 kg/s would be ≈ 99 dB. These are plausible for stationary exhaust measurements but are not sourced values.
- **Why dṁ/dt matters for synthesis:** the derivative gives a +6 dB/octave tilt relative to the mass-flow spectrum; sharp valve-opening "blowdown" fronts therefore dominate the timbre, and any discretisation that smears or aliases those fronts is directly audible.
- **Intake orifice noise** is the same monopole with the sign of ṁ reversed (flow into the snorkel); typically lower level than exhaust because pulse amplitudes are smaller (sub-atmospheric suction waves) and airboxes act as large expansion chambers/Helmholtz resonators.
- **Mechanical sources** (no sources retrieved; textbook/engineering knowledge): gear whine at mesh frequency f_mesh = z·n_shaft/60 (z teeth) and harmonics; turbo compressor blade-pass f_BPF = N_blades·n_turbo/60 (often >10 kHz; audible "whine" also includes shaft-order and sub-synchronous content); piston slap as impacts near TDC/BDC (2 per revolution per cylinder, i.e., order-locked impulsive excitation of block modes); valvetrain impacts at valve-seating angles. All of these are impulsive or tonal inputs into the engine structure that radiate via shell modes.

## 2. Linear acoustics of ducts: plane waves, cut-on, transfer matrices, mufflers, mean flow and temperature

### Takeaway
Below the first cut-on frequency of higher-order modes, exhaust/intake ducts are 1D plane-wave systems that can be described exactly (linearly) by 2×2 transfer (four-pole) matrices; chaining matrices for pipes, expansion chambers, side-branch resonators and perforates gives transmission loss (TL) and, with source and radiation impedances, insertion loss (IL). Mean flow splits the wavenumber into downstream/upstream values k/(1±M), and exhaust temperature raises c to ~450–600 m/s, which shifts every resonance by 30–70% relative to room-temperature air.

### Cited Findings
- TL definition: for negligible mean flow with an anechoic termination and equal inlet/outlet areas, TL = 20 log10(|p_incident|/|p_transmitted|) — [Wikipedia, Transmission loss (duct acoustics)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_loss_(duct_acoustics)).
- A simple expansion chamber has zero-dB TL troughs at f_n = n·c0/(2L), L the chamber length — [Munjal et al., IJAV 2020, two-chamber mufflers](https://www.iiav.org/ijav/content/volumes/25_2020_36521584896523/vol_2/1666_fullpaper_1041381593513084.pdf).
- Munjal derived transfer matrices for plane waves in a moving medium at small Mach numbers (side inlet/outlet chambers) showing how mean flow affects TL; with mean flow the aeroacoustic state variables are total-pressure and mass-velocity perturbations — [Munjal, repository.ias.ac.in 75286](https://repository.ias.ac.in/75286); [J. IISc, Munjal velocity-ratio method](https://journal.iisc.ac.in/index.php/iisc/article/download/3711/3749). Thawani & Doige used 2×2 transfer matrices with mean flow and damping for pipes and expansion chambers — [J. Can. Acoust. Assoc.](https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/497/168/558).
- Helmholtz resonator: f_H = (c/2π)·√(A/(L·V)), A neck area, L neck length, V cavity volume — [US Patent 11143070 (muffler)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11143070). End corrections quoted vary: L_eff = L + 0.85·D_T in one source vs. 0.6a for an unflanged neck end in another — [Rose-Hulman acoustics notes](https://www.rose-hulman.edu/~moloney/Acoustics/0506/Transmission_branches_matrices_06.doc). Maximal attenuation occurs at resonance (spring = cavity, mass = neck fluid) — [Siemens STAR-CCM+ KB](https://support.sw.siemens.com/en-US/okba/KB000112186_EN_US/How-to-calculate-the-Transmission-Loss-of-a-Helmholtz-resonator/index.html). Side branches give high TL in a narrow band and are combined with straight sections by transfer-matrix products — [UPB Sci. Bull. paper](https://www.scientificbulletin.upb.ro/static/pdfs/rez276_281561.pdf). Mean flow alters resonator behaviour — [COMSOL "Helmholtz resonator with flow"](https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.aco.helmholtz_resonator_with_flow/helmholtz_resonator_with_flow.html).
- Muffler metric definitions (TL, IL, noise reduction) and advanced topics are summarised in the University of Kentucky Vibro-Acoustics Consortium webinar — [UKY VAC "Muffler Metrics – Advanced Topics"](https://vac.engr.uky.edu/sites/vac/files/Webinars/07_VAC_Muffler_Metrics_Advanced_Topics.pdf).

### Inferences
All formulas in this block are **[textbook, not re-verified]** (Munjal *Acoustics of Ducts and Mufflers* 2nd ed. 2014 ch. 2–3; Kinsler et al. ch. 10; Pierce ch. 7) unless they are my arithmetic.
- **Speed of sound / temperature:** c = √(γ R_s T). Air 293 K: 343 m/s. Exhaust gas (γ ≈ 1.33–1.37, R_s ≈ 287–290 J/kg/K): 500 K → ≈ 443 m/s; 800 K → ≈ 557 m/s; 900 K → ≈ 586 m/s (my arithmetic). Density ρ = p/(R_s T) falls correspondingly, so Z = ρc/S drops with temperature.
- **Plane-wave propagation in a uniform pipe:** p(x,t) = p⁺(t − x/c) + p⁻(t + x/c); volume velocity U = (p⁺ − p⁻)/Z. With mean flow speed u (M = u/c), the waves travel at c+u and c−u: k± = k/(1 ± M). Delays: τ⁺ = L/(c+u), τ⁻ = L/(c−u). Open-open pipe resonances shift to f_n = n·c(1−M²)/(2L).
- **Cut-on of first non-planar mode** in a circular duct: f_c = 1.841·c/(π d) (first (1,1) mode), i.e., plane-wave theory is valid for k·a < 1.841. Examples (my arithmetic): 50 mm header at c = 550 m/s → f_c ≈ 6.4 kHz (plane waves are fine almost to Nyquist of a 10–20 kHz simulation); 300 mm muffler shell at c = 450 m/s → f_c ≈ 0.88 kHz (3D effects inside mufflers above ~1 kHz; 1D models underpredict/overpredict TL there). With mean flow f_c is lowered by √(1−M²).
- **Four-pole matrix of a uniform pipe (no flow):** [p1; U1] = [[cos kL, jZ sin kL], [j sin kL / Z, cos kL]]·[p2; U2]. With low-M mean flow (Munjal): multiply by e^{−jMk_cL} and use k_c = k/(1−M²) in the trig terms. Lumped elements: side branch of impedance Z_b → [[1, 0], [1/Z_b, 1]]; series element Z_s → [[1, Z_s], [0, 1]]. Helmholtz branch Z_b = jωρ l_eff/A + ρc²/(jωV) (+ resistance). Quarter-wave side branch Z_b = −jZ_b0 cot(kL_b).
- **TL from the overall matrix T (areas S_in = S_out = S, Z = ρc/S):** TL = 20 log10(½|T11 + T12/Z + Z·T21 + T22|). For a simple expansion chamber of area ratio m = S_chamber/S_pipe and length L: TL = 10 log10[1 + ¼(m − 1/m)² sin²(kL)] (the formula in the brief; it reproduces the cited troughs at kL = nπ, i.e., f = n c/2L). Peak TL at kL = π/2: m = 9 → 13.2 dB; m = 16 → 18.1 dB (my arithmetic). Above f_c of the chamber the real TL deviates from this.
- **Insertion loss** needs source impedance Z_s (engine side) and radiation impedance Z_r: IL = 20 log10 |(T11 Z_r + T12 + T21 Z_s Z_r + T22 Z_s)/(T'11 Z_r + T'12 + T'21 Z_s Z_r + T'22 Z_s)| comparing muffler (T) vs. reference pipe (T'). Because the engine source impedance is time-varying and nonlinear (valves opening/closing), IL from linear theory is approximate — one argument for coupling to the time-domain gas model.
- **Helmholtz example (my arithmetic):** neck d = 40 mm (A = 1.26e-3 m²), l = 50 mm, l_eff ≈ 50 + 2·0.85·20 = 84 mm (flanged corrections both ends), V = 2 L, c = 450 m/s → f_H ≈ 196 Hz. Quarter-wave of an 0.8 m primary at 550 m/s: c/4L ≈ 172 Hz.
- **Perforated tubes and absorptive mufflers** [textbook, not re-verified]: perforate transfer impedance (Sullivan & Crocker type) ζ_p = [6e-3 + jk(t_w + 0.75 d_h)]/σ_p (σ_p porosity, t_w wall thickness, d_h hole diameter), modified by grazing flow; fibrous fill via Delany–Bazley: Z_c = ρc[1 + 0.0571X^−0.754 − j0.087X^−0.732], k_c = k[1 + 0.0978X^−0.700 − j0.189X^−0.595], X = ρf/σ (σ flow resistivity). These enter as distributed coupled-duct matrices (Munjal ch. 3/5). For real time they are best handled offline (compute the system FRF → IR) rather than inside the time loop.
- **Visco-thermal wall losses** [textbook]: α ≈ (1/(a c))·√(πfν)·(1 + (γ−1)/√Pr) Np/m — grows as √f, implementable as a low-order lowpass lumped at the end of each waveguide section (see §5).

## 3. Radiation from an open pipe end: impedance, end correction, reflection coefficient, directivity, far-field pressure

### Takeaway
At the tailpipe and intake mouth almost all low-frequency energy is reflected back (|R| ≈ 1, phase ≈ π, plus an end correction ≈ 0.61a unflanged / 0.82a flanged), and only a small, strongly high-pass-filtered part is radiated; the radiated far field is a monopole p = ρ0/(4πr)·dQ_out/dt until ka ≈ 1, after which directivity and |R| → 0 matter. These behaviours are compactly implementable as a 1st/2nd-order reflection filter at the end of a waveguide plus a differentiator/1/r/delay for the far field.

### Cited Findings
- Levine & Schwinger (1948) solved radiation from an unflanged circular pipe (Wiener–Hopf); the low-frequency end correction is 0.6133a — [Phys. Rev. 73, 383 (1948)](https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRev.73.383); still treated as the classical benchmark in 2025 — [Acta Acustica 2025, aacus250133](https://acta-acustica.edpsciences.org/articles/aacus/full_html/2025/01/aacus250133/aacus250133.html).
- Static end-correction values 0.6133a (unflanged) and 0.8216a (flanged); numerical evaluation of the Levine–Schwinger integral for R(ka) agrees with asymptotic approximations within ~3%; the paper provides approximation formulae for radiation impedance usable in models — [Silva, Guillemain, Kergomard, Mallaroni, Norris, arXiv:0811.3625](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/0811.3625).
- Frequency-dependent refinement: δ(ka) = a(0.6133 − 0.1168(ka)²) (Davies et al.) — [Acta Acustica 2025](https://acta-acustica.edpsciences.org/articles/aacus/full_html/2025/01/aacus250133/aacus250133.html).
- With mean (outflow) the end correction moves from ≈0.61a at high Strouhal number toward ≈0.19a at low Strouhal number — [Acta Acustica 2025](https://acta-acustica.edpsciences.org/10.1051/aacus/2025058); low-Mach, low-Helmholtz-number reflection/damping measurements for an open pipe with flow — [J. Fluid Mech. (Cambridge)](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-fluid-mechanics/article/abs/damping-and-reflection-coefficient-measurements-for-an-open-pipe-at-low-mach-and-low-helmholtz-numbers/176F64F8EDC5D7B2E8C2777D6FAA6FE2).
- A recomputed unflanged value of 0.6127a has been reported (single, less-established source) — [naukaru.ru](https://naukaru.ru/en/nauka/article/12829/view).

### Inferences
- **Low-ka forms [textbook, not re-verified; Levine & Schwinger; Kinsler ch. 7]:** normalised radiation impedance Z_r S/(ρc) ≈ ¼(ka)² + j0.6133ka (unflanged), ≈ ½(ka)² + j0.8216ka (flanged). Reflection coefficient R = −|R|e^{−2jkδ}, |R| ≈ 1 − ½(ka)² (unflanged, ka ≪ 1) — the radiated power fraction is ≈ (ka)²/2. Example: a = 25 mm, ka = 1 at f = c_air/(2πa) ≈ 2.2 kHz (343 m/s outside air; inside-gas c matters for k in the reflection, outside c for the far field). So below ~1 kHz the tailpipe reflects >90% of incident energy, giving the strong pipe resonances heard in exhaust note.
- **Implementation:** fit a low-order IIR R(z) to Levine–Schwinger (−1 at DC, magnitude rolling off, delay ≈ 2δ/c), as done for wind-instrument bells in waveguide synthesis (JOS PASP; cited in §5). The transmitted volume velocity U_out = (p⁺ − p⁻)/Z at the termination, and the far field p_far(t) = ρ_air/(4πr)·dU_out/dt(t − r/c_air) for ka < 1. Equivalent: transmission filter T(z) = 1 + R(z) on pressure feeding a differentiator. Above ka ≈ 1 add a directivity filter (on-axis boost, ≈ −10 dB at 90° side-on for ka ~ 3, from the L–S directivity) if listener angle matters; for car tailpipes (a ≈ 25–40 mm) this only affects > 2–3 kHz.
- **Hot jet / flow effects:** the outflow reduces the end correction (cited) and adds vortex-shedding absorption at low Strouhal number, damping the low-order pipe resonances; a single scalar loss factor on |R| that depends on M is a cheap approximation.

### Gaps
- The explicit Levine–Schwinger R(ka) integral and directivity curves were not retrieved; implementers should take rational-fit coefficients directly from Silva et al. (arXiv:0811.3625).

## 4. Non-linear (finite-amplitude) exhaust waves and coupling 1D gas dynamics to radiation

### Takeaway
Exhaust blowdown pulses are finite-amplitude waves (large fraction of an atmosphere near the valve) that steepen, can form shocks, and propagate at local speed u ± c depending on local temperature; linear acoustics mispredicts both their propagation and muffler performance, which is why the industry standard is 1D unsteady gas dynamics (method of characteristics, later finite-volume). The physically correct coupling to sound is to take the mass flow (or particle velocity × area × density) at the open tailpipe/intake cell of the 1D solver, differentiate it, and radiate it as a monopole.

### Cited Findings
- Exhaust noise originates from wave action after cylinder blowdown; "there are, however, serious limitations in the applicability of linear theories; the wave action produces large amplitude pressure waves, particularly upstream in the muffler system"; linear theory cannot capture large-amplitude steepening; Jones & Brown computed two-stroke exhaust noise by the method of characteristics (finite sets of C+, C−, particle paths) and observed wave steepening and shock formation, differing from Blair's and Karnopp et al.'s approaches — [NASA NTRS 19820005954](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19820005954/downloads/19820005954.pdf); [TRID 181750](https://trid.trb.org/View/181750).
- For a finite-amplitude pulsive flow in a straight pipe, linear acoustic estimates of muffler noise reduction disagreed with experiment, while the method of characteristics agreed fairly well — [IoA, Okda & Okumura](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/J%20OKDA%20Y%20OKUMURA%20PROPAGATION%20OF%20A%20PULSIVE%20FLOW%20WITH%20FINITE%20AMPLITUDE%20IN%20A%20STRAIGHT%20PIPE.pdf).
- Nonlinear duct problems are usually solved by characteristics or finite-difference/volume methods; conventional FD/FV schemes can be unstable/inaccurate for the small-amplitude waves that make up much of the pulse, motivating a pressure-correction method — [IoA, Titahmboh & Peat](https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/publications/MM%20TITAHMBOH%20KS%20PEAT%20PRESSURE%20CORRECTION%20METHOD%20FOR%20NON-LINEAR%20DUCT%20ACOUSTICS.pdf).
- Gupta & Munjal (1993): time-domain finite-wave analysis of exhaust systems is usually done by the stationary-frame method of characteristics, with good agreement with experiment — [Springer doi 10.1007/BF03028386](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/BF03028386).
- Commercial 1D engine codes (e.g., WAVE) break the flow system into 1D elements; a real-time variant (WAVE-RT) exists for HIL controls work, not audio — [MathWorks connections: WAVE](https://www.mathworks.com/products/connections/product_detail/wave.html). Coupled 1D–3D CFD is used for exhaust-noise optimisation of a single-cylinder engine — [ResearchGate 345783278](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/345783278_Exhaust_Noise_Optimization_for_a_Single_Cylinder_Engine_by_1D_-_3D_CFD_Coupled_Simulation_Approach).

### Inferences
- **Governing equations (quasi-1D Euler with area change, friction and heat transfer) [textbook]:** ∂(ρS)/∂t + ∂(ρuS)/∂x = 0; ∂(ρuS)/∂t + ∂((ρu² + p)S)/∂x = p dS/dx − F_f; ∂(ρES)/∂t + ∂(ρuHS)/∂x = q̇S. Riemann invariants (isentropic): J± = u ± 2c/(γ−1) carried at u ± c — a wave crest (higher p, T, c, u) travels faster than its trough, giving steepening; shock formation distance x_s ≈ 2γ p0 λ /((γ+1)·2π·Δp) for a sinusoid of amplitude Δp (plane-wave Burgers estimate).
- **When nonlinearity matters (my reasoning):** Δp/p0 near the exhaust port at WOT is order 0.3–1 (Blair's books show port pressures exceeding 1.5–2 bar abs at blowdown — not retrieved here, see Gaps); at such ratios wave speed varies by ±10–25% within a pulse, so header tuning (when the reflected rarefaction returns to the port) shifts with load — linear constant-c delay lines cannot capture that. Downstream of the first muffler, Δp/p0 is typically a few percent, so linear propagation is acceptable there. This suggests a **hybrid**: nonlinear 1D near the valves/headers/collector, linear waveguide/transfer-matrix downstream.
- **Source extraction for audio:** at the open end cell, ṁ_out(t) = ρ_e u_e S_e (boundary face flux of the 1D solver). The radiated pressure is p(r,t) = (1/4πr)·dṁ_out/dt delayed by r/c_air. Compute the derivative on the band-limited (resampled) signal, not at the raw solver step, to avoid amplifying numerical noise; equivalently radiate the band-limited flux through a first-order differentiator (1 − z⁻¹)·f_a.
- **Grid/rate feasibility (my arithmetic):** explicit FV CFL: Δt ≤ Δx/(|u|+c). With |u|+c ≈ 700 m/s: Δx = 20 mm → Δt ≤ 28.6 µs (≥ 35 kHz); Δx = 50 mm → Δt ≤ 71 µs (≥ 14 kHz). A 2nd-order MUSCL-Hancock/HLLC scheme resolves roughly ≥ 8–10 cells per wavelength, so f_max ≈ c/(10Δx): 2.75 kHz at 20 mm, 1.1 kHz at 50 mm. A V8 with 8 × 0.8 m primaries and 2 × 3 m of downstream pipe at Δx = 20 mm ≈ 620 cells; at ~150–300 flops/cell/step and 40 kHz that is ≈ 4–7 GFLOP/s — too heavy for one core alongside the rest of engine-sim, but ~5× cheaper at Δx = 50 mm/15 kHz. Hence: nonlinear 1D on coarse grid for the energetic low band (< 1–1.5 kHz, where firing orders and header tuning live) + linear waveguides/IR for higher frequencies.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved quantifies typical exhaust-port pulse amplitudes or the Δp/p0 threshold at which linear acoustics fails; Blair, *Design and Simulation of Four-Stroke Engines* (SAE 1999) is the expected reference.
- No published real-time (audio-rate) 1D gas-dynamics engine sound synthesizer with performance figures was found; WAVE-RT targets controls HIL, not audio.

## 5. Real-time synthesis approaches and DSP details (waveguides, WDF, convolution, granular, papers; fractional delay; SRC; anti-aliasing)

### Takeaway
Three families exist: (a) sample/granular/PSOLA playback of recordings (commercial games; Jagla et al.), cheap and realistic but not physically controllable; (b) physically informed procedural models — pulse trains or per-cylinder pulses through digital waveguides/resonators (Farnell → Baldan et al. 2015; Doerfler & Wyse 2026 differentiable PTR); (c) physics simulation → source signal → linear filter (engine-sim: lumped 0D gas model + recorded impulse-response convolution). Waveguides with fractional-delay interpolation are the natural real-time bridge between a 1D gas model and audio because they are exact solutions of the linear 1D wave equation at one delay-line read per sample.

### Cited Findings
- Baldan, Lachambre, Delle Monache & Boussard, "Physically informed car engine sound synthesis for virtual and augmented environments", SIVE@IEEE VR 2015, pp. 21–26: procedural, physics-based, positioned as an efficient, flexible alternative to sample-based and analysis/resynthesis approaches; implemented as a Max/MSP external, integrated in GeneCars (driving simulator for industrial sound design) and SkAT Studio — [dblp](https://dblp.dagstuhl.de/pid/16/8243.html); [SIVE 2015](https://sive.create.aau.dk/index.php/sive-2015/); [ResearchGate 280086598](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/280086598_Physically_informed_car_engine_sound_synthesis_for_virtual_and_augmented_environments). Its core is a digital waveguide (published block diagram) and it builds on Andy Farnell's model, whose premise is that the salient acoustic contribution of a 4-stroke is the pulsed exhaust gas — [ResearchGate figure "Block diagram of the digital waveguide"](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Block-diagram-of-the-digital-waveguide-implemented-in-the-current-version-of-the-engine_fig4_280086598) (via search summary; full text not read).
- Jagla, Maillard & Martin, "Sample-based engine noise synthesis using an enhanced pitch-synchronous overlap-and-add method", JASA 132(5):3098–3108 (2012), doi 10.1121/1.4754663: extracts one-engine-cycle grains at instants located using the engine's periodicity and joins them by overlap-add; high quality at low computational load, suitable for real time — [HAL hal-00730614](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-00730614).
- Doerfler & Wyse (2026), "Physics-Informed Neural Engine Sound Modeling with Differentiable Pulse-Train Synthesis" (arXiv:2603.09391): parameterised pulse trains aligned to firing patterns, passed through recursive Karplus–Strong resonators standing in for exhaust acoustics; reported 21% better harmonic reconstruction and 5.7% lower total loss than a harmonic-plus-noise baseline, trained on ~2.5 h of procedurally generated engine audio — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09391); [Recercat thesis record](https://recercat.cat/handle/10230/72480).
- Patents describe sound-synthesis devices imparting a "simulated exhaust-pipe characteristic" to an exhaust waveform — [US 5835605](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5835605); and PSOLA-like engine pitch scaling — [US 12243510](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12243510).
- Julius O. Smith, *Physical Audio Signal Processing* (CCRMA, online): digital waveguides, allpass filters, delay-line interpolation including Lagrange interpolation and fractional-delay filter design — [PASP](https://www.dsprelated.com/freebooks/pasp/); Thiran allpass interpolator code and group-delay plots (an "easy zone" with delay ≈ order + 0.3 samples) — [JOS Thiran allpass](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Thiran_Allpass_Interpolation_Matlab.html), [Thiran group delays](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp05/Group_Delays_Thiran_Allpass.html); closed waveguide networks realise MIMO allpass filters, with FDNs as a special case — [JOS allpass waveguide networks](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp05/Allpass_Digital_Waveguide_Networks.html).
- Lagrange interpolators and Thiran allpass filters both arise from Padé-type approximations of the ideal fractional delay (series vs. continued fraction) — [Tassart & Depalle, ICASSP 1997](https://www2.spsc.tugraz.at/people/franklyn/ICASSP97/pdf/author/ic970455.pdf). Välimäki's "Discrete-time modeling of acoustic tubes using fractional delay filters" is the canonical tube reference — [Wikipedia "Digital delay line" refs](https://wikipedia.com/wiki/Digital_delay).
- A hobbyist forum claims engine-sim plays back a pre-recorded sample pitched with rpm — [gamedev.net "Procedural engine audio"](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/713424-procedural-engine-audio/); this is **contradicted by the code** (see §8): the recorded WAVs are used as convolution impulse responses, not as pitched loops — [local es/sound-library/impulse_responses.mr](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/es/sound-library/impulse_responses.mr).

### Inferences
- **Digital waveguide pipe (exact for linear lossless 1D):** two delay lines of N = L·f_a/(c ± u) samples (fractional), each followed by a loss filter H_loss(z) approximating e^{−α(f)L}; junctions between pipes of areas S_i use the pressure scattering junction p_J = 2Σ_i Y_i p_i⁺ / Σ_i Y_i, Y_i = S_i/(ρ_i c_i), outgoing p_i⁻ = p_J − p_i⁺ (Kelly–Lochbaum generalisation; JOS PASP) [textbook, not re-verified]. Terminations: open end → R(z) (§3), closed valve → +1, open valve → coupling to the cylinder volume (0D) through the valve flow equation (a nonlinear junction).
- **Temperature/flow-dependent sound speed:** delay length N(t) = L·f_a/(c(T(t)) ± u(t)) must be time-varying; use 3rd-order Lagrange (4 taps) or Thiran 1st-order allpass for the fractional part. Lagrange is cheap and stable under modulation but low-passes at high frequency; Thiran is flat in magnitude but gives transients when its coefficient is modulated quickly — so smooth N(t) (e.g., one-pole at ~10–50 Hz) and prefer Lagrange in the forward path. Sample a representative T per pipe from the gas model (engine-sim already tracks per-volume temperature).
- **Wave digital filters** (Fettweis) are an alternative for lumped elements (Helmholtz cavities, valve orifices as nonlinear resistors) with guaranteed passivity; they connect naturally to waveguides via wave variables. No WDF engine-sound paper was found in this session.
- **Convolution (engine-sim's approach):** uniformly partitioned FFT convolution of a ≤10,000-tap IR at 44.1 kHz with B = 128 costs O(log B + P) per sample (P = 79 partitions) — a few tens of MFLOP/s per channel; negligible. Its limitation is not cost but physics: an IR is linear, time-invariant, fixed-temperature, and if it is a recording, unrelated to the simulated geometry.
- **Physically derived IRs (cheap upgrade):** compute the frequency response of the downstream linear system (muffler + tailpipe + radiation + listener distance) from the four-pole chain of §2/§3 on a dense frequency grid, inverse-FFT to an IR (minimum-phase or with exact delays), and load it into the existing ConvolutionFilter. Cost: milliseconds, re-run when geometry/temperature changes (crossfade old/new IR).
- **Sample-rate conversion and anti-aliasing:** physics at f_sim (6.5–20 kHz in engine-sim) must be resampled to f_a = 44.1/48 kHz with a time-varying ratio (simulation-speed slider). Linear interpolation's image suppression is only sinc²(f/f_sim): with f_sim = 10 kHz an image of a 4 kHz tone lands at 6 kHz attenuated by only ≈ 12 dB (my arithmetic) — audible "metallic" imaging. Use a polyphase windowed-sinc ASRC (32–64 taps, Kaiser window) — ~100–200 MACs/sample/channel. Alternatively run the acoustic part (waveguides) natively at f_a and only resample the band-limited source (valve mass flow), which is smoother. Discontinuities at valve opening in a 0D/1D solver alias at the physics rate itself; mitigate by computing the solver at 2× and decimating, or by smoothing valve-lift edges (they are smooth cam profiles physically).
- **Granular/sample approach for comparison:** order-tracked recordings resynthesised by PSOLA (Jagla) or crossfaded rpm/load loops (common in games) give realistic timbre with ~zero physics, but cannot represent new geometry, cylinder misfire, or header changes — the opposite trade-off from engine-sim's aim.

- Full texts of Baldan et al. (2015) and Farnell (*Designing Sound*, MIT Press 2010) were not accessible; exact waveguide topology, parameter values and CPU cost of Baldan's model are unverified.

## 6. Structural/mechanical contributions and listener perspective (cabin vs exterior, Doppler, distance, air absorption)

### Takeaway
Exterior sound is dominated by tailpipe/intake monopoles plus shell radiation, filtered by 1/r spreading, propagation delay (with Doppler for moving sources), ground reflection and — only at long distances — atmospheric absorption; in-cabin sound is dominated by low engine orders transmitted structure-borne (mounts) and airborne (firewall, floor), shaped by cabin modes, so a cabin listener hears much less high-frequency exhaust content and more firing-order "boom".

### Cited Findings
- Atmospheric absorption (ISO 9613-1:1993) is specified as pure-tone attenuation vs. frequency (50 Hz–10 kHz), temperature (−20…+50 °C), humidity (10–100%) and pressure; arises mainly from O₂ and N₂ molecular relaxation catalysed by water vapour; coefficient in dB/m = 8.686·f²·[classical + O₂ relaxation + N₂ relaxation terms] with relaxation frequencies f_rO, f_rN depending on p, T, humidity — [sengpielaudio ISO 9613-1 formula transcription](https://sengpielaudio.com/AirdampingFormula.htm); [ISO 9613-1 preview (SAI Global)](https://saiglobal.com/PDFTemp/Previews/OSH/ISO/IPDF0032/T017426E.PDF); [SCC record](https://scc-ccn.ca/standardsdb/standards/8107972).
- Pass-by homologation measures at 7.5 m with microphones each side of the vehicle path (defines the canonical "exterior listener") — [B&K pass-by](https://www.bksv.com/en/knowledge/blog/sound/pass-by-noise-regulations).

### Inferences
- **Free-field propagation per source [textbook]:** p_L(t) = s(t − r(t)/c_air)/r(t) with s the source term (e.g., ṁ' derivative/4π). Implement as a variable fractional delay line (Lagrange-3) whose read position follows r(t)/c_air — this yields Doppler automatically (f' = f·c/(c − v_r) for an approaching source) without explicit pitch shifting (JOS PASP variable delay lines). Smooth r(t) to avoid zipper noise.
- **Ground reflection:** add an image source at mirror height with reflection factor ≈ 0.8–1 for asphalt; produces comb filtering that moves with distance — cheap (one extra delay tap per source).
- **Air absorption** at ~20 °C/70% RH is of order 5 dB/km at 1 kHz and tens of dB/km at 4–8 kHz (approximate recollection of ISO 9613-1 tables; not verified): negligible at 7.5 m, relevant beyond ~100 m. Implement as a one-pole lowpass whose cutoff falls with distance, or a shelving filter fit to ISO 9613-1 at the current r.
- **Multiple sources and spatial separation:** tailpipe(s) at the rear, intake and shell at the front; for exterior views the relative delays/levels between them change with listener position and create order-dependent interference (dual exhausts).
- **Cabin:** model as source → transfer path FIRs/IIRs (firewall mass-law TL rising ~6 dB/octave; mount/body transfer for orders 2–6 below ~300 Hz) → cabin resonance bank (first longitudinal mode of a car cabin roughly c/(2·L_cabin) ≈ 343/(2·2.5) ≈ 70 Hz, my arithmetic). Engineering practice is "source–transfer path–receiver" (TPA); no sourced numeric transfer functions were found.
- **Structure-borne/combustion synthesis (real-time feasible):** take simulated cylinder pressure p_cyl(t) per cylinder; high-pass/derivative (combustion noise scales with pressure-rise rate) → bank of 10–30 modal resonators (2nd-order IIRs at block/oil-pan/cover modes, roughly 0.5–4 kHz, Q ≈ 10–50) → shell radiation gain; mechanical impacts (piston slap at TDC/BDC, valve seating at closing angles) as short impulses injected into the same modal bank; gear/turbo whine as order-locked sinusoids with amplitude from load/boost. All of this is a few hundred MACs/sample.

### Gaps
- No sourced modal frequencies/damping for engine blocks, transfer functions for cabin paths, or quantitative cabin-vs-exterior spectra were retrieved.
- Exact ISO 9613-1 constants and table values were not verified (only a secondary transcription).

### Inferences
- **Concrete test harness for engine-sim (my proposal):** (1) offline render of a slow 1000→redline WOT sweep, logging crank angle with each audio sample; (2) angle-domain resampling (computed order tracking) → order magnitudes vs rpm for orders 0.5…12 in 0.5 steps; (3) overall dB(A) vs rpm (A-weighting IIR); (4) 1/3-octave spectra at 2000/4000/6000 rpm; (5) compare against recordings of the same engine type (order structure and relative order levels are robust targets even when absolute level/microphone position differ); (6) for the gas model, compare runner/tailpipe pressure vs crank angle against an open-source 1D engine code (e.g., OpenWAM — not researched here) or published traces.
- Unit tests for DSP blocks: expansion-chamber TL from the waveguide network vs the closed-form TL formula; open-pipe resonance frequencies vs n·c/(2(L + 2δ)); radiation |R|(ka) vs Silva et al. fits; Doppler shift vs analytic for a constant-velocity pass.

## 8. Assessment of engine-sim's audio pipeline and what a more physical pipeline would look like

### Takeaway
engine-sim generates its audio from a lumped (0D) exhaust-runner gas volume pressure, not from a tailpipe mass-flow derivative; it applies a fixed integer-sample delay computed at an assumed 343 m/s and an assumed 10 kHz rate, sums cylinders per exhaust system with a 1/L² gain, linearly upsamples with a 1.9 kHz low-pass, multiplies by low-passed noise, and convolves with a recorded WAV impulse response. This is an effective artistic pipeline but several steps are non-physical, and a few are plain inconsistencies that are cheap to fix. A more physical pipeline replaces the recorded IR with a geometry-derived waveguide/transfer-matrix network driven by valve mass flow and radiates dṁ/dt at the open ends.

### Cited Findings (primary source: local code, read in this session)
- Source signal per cylinder = `attenuation³ · 1600 · [(p_runner − 1 atm) + 0.1·dynP(+1) + 0.1·dynP(−1)]`, where `p_runner` is the pressure of the lumped `m_exhaustRunnerAndPrimary` GasSystem and `attenuation = min(|rpm_filtered|, 40)/40` — [src/piston_engine_simulator.cpp:419-458](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/piston_engine_simulator.cpp).
- Per-cylinder delay = (header primary length + exhaust length)/343 m/s, rounded to an integer number of samples using a hard-coded 10,000 Hz rate (`m_delayFilters[i].initialize(delay, 10000.0)`) — [src/piston_engine_simulator.cpp:252-259](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/piston_engine_simulator.cpp); DelayFilter rounds to whole samples — [include/delay_filter.h](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/include/delay_filter.h). Engine scripts set `simulation_frequency` between 6500 and 20000 Hz (grep of `assets/engines` and `es/`), and the synthesizer's input rate is `simulationFrequency × simulationSpeed` — [src/simulator.cpp:87,228](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/simulator.cpp).
- Each cylinder's delayed signal is scaled by `soundAttenuation × audioVolume / cylinderCount × 1/exhaustLength²` and summed into one channel per exhaust system — [src/piston_engine_simulator.cpp:450-457](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/piston_engine_simulator.cpp).
- The exhaust system itself is a lumped GasSystem with an outlet flow constant to an atmosphere reservoir; `ExhaustSystem::process` stores the outlet flow (moles transferred per step) in `m_flow`, exposed as `getFlow()`, which is not used by the audio path — [src/exhaust_system.cpp:62-86](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/exhaust_system.cpp); [src/gas_system.cpp:360-430](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/gas_system.cpp).
- `Synthesizer::writeInput` linearly interpolates from the simulation rate to 44.1 kHz and passes each sample through a Butterworth low-pass with 1900 Hz cutoff — [src/synthesizer.cpp writeInput / initialize](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/synthesizer.cpp).
- `renderBlock`: random fractional-delay "jitter" (up to 10 samples), 10 Hz DC removal, then v = dF_F_mix·dx/dt + (1 − dF_F_mix)·x·(airNoise·LPF₂ₖ(noise) + 1 − airNoise), defaults dF_F_mix = 0.01, airNoise = 1.0, airNoiseFrequencyCutoff = 2000 Hz, inputSampleNoise = 0.5 → partitioned FFT convolution (128-sample partitions) with the per-exhaust IR, then a 0.45·f_a low-pass, automatic leveler (target 30000) and int16 clip — [src/synthesizer.cpp renderBlock](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/synthesizer.cpp); [include/synthesizer.h:24-30](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/include/synthesizer.h); [include/jitter_filter.h](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/include/jitter_filter.h); [include/convolution_filter.h](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/include/convolution_filter.h).
- IRs are recorded 16-bit WAVs from a sound library (e.g., `smooth/smooth_39.wav`, volume 0.001), truncated after the last sample with |x| > 100 and capped at 10,000 taps — [es/sound-library/impulse_responses.mr](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/es/sound-library/impulse_responses.mr); [src/synthesizer.cpp initializeImpulseResponse](https://github.com/ange-yaghi/engine-sim/blob/master/src/synthesizer.cpp).
- Press coverage describes engine-sim as simulating the physics (crank geometry, chamber volume, gas laws, exhaust flow) rather than playing recordings and feeding outputs through "a complex audio approximation system" — [The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/new-3d-engine-sim-can-synthesize-super-realistic-exhaust-sounds-in-real-time).

### Inferences
**Assessment of the current pipeline (my analysis of the code above):**
1. *Source quantity.* Runner gauge pressure is used instead of the radiating quantity (dṁ/dt at the tailpipe). Since dF_F_mix = 0.01, ~99% of the excitation is pressure × noise; the derivative term that would correspond to a monopole is nearly unused. The runner pressure is a reasonable proxy for blowdown timing and shape, but it does not see muffler/tailpipe dynamics because downstream propagation is replaced by the IR.
2. *Delay bug/inconsistency.* The delay in samples is computed with a fixed 10 kHz rate while the filter is clocked at f_sim (6.5–20 kHz) and, for the user, at f_sim × simulationSpeed: actual delay = (L/343)·(10000/f_sim). At 20 kHz the delays are halved; at 6.5 kHz they are 54% too long. Delay is also integer-rounded (100 µs steps at 10 kHz ≈ 3.4 cm of pipe at 343 m/s) and uses room-temperature c (true exhaust c ≈ 450–600 m/s, so physical delays are ~25–45% shorter). Fix: initialise with the actual f_sim and c(T), and use a fractional-delay read.
3. *Delay meaning.* A single pure delay per cylinder models only the first arrival at the tailpipe; reflections (header tuning, collector, muffler) are absent from the time domain and supplied implicitly (and geometry-independently) by the recorded IR.
4. *1/L² gain* has no acoustic basis (pipe length changes resonances and visco-thermal loss, not geometric spreading); it acts as an artistic per-cylinder balance.
5. *Bandwidth.* Linear interpolation + 1.9 kHz low-pass means all simulated content above ~2 kHz is discarded; high-frequency character comes from the noise modulation (LPF 2 kHz) and the IR. This hides imaging/aliasing from the 10 kHz physics rate but caps physical bandwidth well below the plane-wave limit (≈ 6 kHz in a 50 mm pipe).
6. *Noise multiplication* (amplitude-modulated noise) is a perceptual model of turbulent flow noise during blowdown — physically motivated (flow noise scales with flow speed) but not derived from the gas state; a more physical choice is noise amplitude ∝ (u_exit)^n with n ≈ 3–4 for dipole/quadrupole-like scaling (approximate, unsourced) gated by actual outflow.
7. *Leveler* (automatic gain) removes the absolute SPL vs rpm/load relationship, which defeats SPL-vs-rpm validation; it should be bypassable in an "engineering" mode.
8. *Positives:* per-cylinder sources preserved (half orders and firing-order character survive); efficient partitioned FFT convolution; thread separation of audio from physics.

**A more physical real-time pipeline (proposed; costs are my estimates):**
- **Stage A — source.** Per cylinder, exhaust-valve mass flow ṁ_v(t) [kg/s] = (moles per step / dt)·M_molar from the existing valve flow, plus port gas temperature T_v. Same for intake valves (negative). Runs at f_sim.
- **Stage B — header/runner (nonlinear-capable).** Option B1 (cheap, linear): per-primary waveguide pair, delay L/(c(T) ± u) with Lagrange-3 fractional delays, loss filter, closed/open valve termination coupling to the 0D cylinder; Option B2 (accurate): coarse quasi-1D finite-volume solver (Δx ≈ 40–50 mm, MUSCL-Hancock + HLLC or Lax–Wendroff with flux limiter, ~15–20 cells per primary) run at f_sim ≥ 15 kHz — captures pulse steepening and load-dependent tuning in the < 1–1.5 kHz band; ≈ 0.3–1 GFLOP/s for a V8.
- **Stage C — collector and downstream (linear).** Waveguide network (collector = N-port scattering junction; muffler as expansion-chamber sections, Helmholtz branches as 2nd-order resonators; resonator/perforate effects via precomputed short IRs). Or compute the four-pole chain FRF of everything downstream of the collector offline → IR → existing ConvolutionFilter (re-derived when geometry or mean exhaust temperature changes). This keeps engine-sim's convolution machinery but makes the IR geometry-derived instead of a recording.
- **Stage D — termination and radiation.** Levine–Schwinger R(z) at each tailpipe/intake mouth (low-order IIR); radiated far field p = ρ_air/(4πr)·dU_out/dt (or (1/4πr)·dṁ_out/dt) with r/c_air delay.
- **Stage E — structure/mechanical.** Cylinder-pressure-derivative-driven modal bank + impact events + order-locked tonal components (§6).
- **Stage F — listener.** Per-source variable delay (Doppler), 1/r, ground image source, ISO 9613-1 air-absorption shelf for long distances; optional cabin transfer filters; binaural/HRTF optional.
- **Stage G — rate conversion.** Either run Stages C–F at f_a (waveguides are cheapest at audio rate) and up-convert only Stage A/B outputs with a polyphase windowed-sinc ASRC, or run the whole acoustic stage at f_sim and ASRC the final mix. Replace linear interpolation + 1.9 kHz LPF with a proper ASRC; keep a guard band (f_sim ≥ 2.5× highest physically resolved frequency).
- **Budget (estimate):** Stages C–F for a V8 with dual exhaust: ~16 waveguide pairs × ~20 ops + 2 radiation + 30 modal IIRs + ASRC ≈ 2–5 k ops/sample ≈ 0.1–0.25 GFLOP/s at 48 kHz — comfortably real-time on one core; Stage B2 is the only expensive part and scales with grid resolution.
- **Incremental path for engine-sim (lowest risk first):** (1) fix delay initialisation to actual f_sim and use c(T_exhaust) and fractional delay; (2) offer `ExhaustSystem::getFlow()`-based dṁ/dt as an alternative source (weight via existing dF_F_mix-like parameter); (3) replace linear interpolation with a windowed-sinc resampler and raise the 1.9 kHz cutoff toward 0.45·f_sim; (4) generate IRs from exhaust geometry via transfer matrices; (5) add per-primary waveguides with collector junction; (6) only then consider a 1D FV solver for headers.
